\begin{remindbox}
\small
\textbf{Bias is a direction, not bad luck.} Honest random samples scatter
\emph{around} the truth; a biased study misses the \emph{same way every time}.
It gets in two places. Before anyone speaks: \textbf{undercoverage} (left off the
frame) and \textbf{nonresponse} (chosen but silent). After the right person is
chosen: \textbf{response bias} --- demand, social desirability, dissent,
acquiescence, extreme responses, neutral responding, and question order. And the
line worth memorizing: \emph{a larger sample makes an estimate more precise, not
more correct.}
\end{remindbox}
