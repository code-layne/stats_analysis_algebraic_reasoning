\boxguard[24]
\begin{scenariobox}[Harbor Point Community Pool]{cerulean}
\small
The Harbor Point Community Pool has $1{,}500$ members. The board is deciding
whether to reserve a second lane for lap swimming, so a staff member asks
$75$ members arriving on a Tuesday evening what they mainly use the pool for.

\vspace{2pt}
The $75$ answers: $27$ lap swimming, $21$ family swim, $18$ lessons,
$9$ water aerobics.
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.08in}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{Practice}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=9pt, topsep=3pt]

  \item Name the groups in this study.

        Population: \blank{5.5cm} \hfill Sample size: \blank{1.6cm}

        Sample: \blank{6.5cm}

  \item Complete the staff member's frequency table.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{} l c X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Main use} & \textbf{Count} &
        \textbf{Relative frequency (percent)} \\
        \midrule
        Lap swimming   & 27 & \blank{4.4cm} \\
        Family swim    & 21 & \blank{4.4cm} \\
        Lessons        & 18 & \blank{4.4cm} \\
        Water aerobics &  9 & \blank{4.4cm} \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Total} & \blank{1.4cm} & \blank{4.4cm} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

        Show your work for the \textbf{water aerobics} row.

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{9}{75} &= 0.12 \\
                        &= 12\%
        \end{work}

  \item Estimate how many of the pool's $1{,}500$ members mainly swim laps.

        \begin{work}
          0.36 \times 1500 &= 540 \text{ members}
        \end{work}

        Write that result as one sentence for the board. Name the group and say
        that the number is an estimate.
        \writelines{2}

  \item Eight of the members also reported how many times they visited the pool
        last month: $12, \; 4, \; 8, \; 20, \; 6, \; 10, \; 16, \; 4$.

        \textbf{(a)} Find the mean number of visits.

        \begin{work}
          \bar{x} &= \dfrac{12+4+8+20+6+10+16+4}{8} \\
                  &= \dfrac{80}{8} \\
                  &= 10 \text{ visits}
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} Is that $10$ a parameter or a statistic? Say how you know.
        \writeline

  \item Label each number \textbf{parameter} or \textbf{statistic}.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.95\linewidth}{@{} X c @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{The number} & \textbf{Which kind?} \\
        \midrule
        The pool has $1{,}500$ members.                             & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        $36\%$ of the $75$ members asked mainly swim laps.          & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        The true mean number of visits per month for all $1{,}500$ members.
          & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item Name the constraint that keeps the board from surveying all $1{,}500$
        members in each situation.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.97\linewidth}{@{} X p{3cm} @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Situation} & \textbf{Constraint} \\
        \midrule
        Reaching every member in person would take staff three weeks they do not
        have. & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        Mailing a survey to all $1{,}500$ would cost more than the board budgeted.
          & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        Some members moved away and left no forwarding address.
          & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        Members join and quit every month, so the list is never the same twice.
          & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item A month later a different staff member asks another $75$ members the same
        question. This time $30$ say they mainly swim laps.

        \textbf{(a)} What percent of that second sample is that?

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{30}{75} &= 0.40 \\
                         &= 40\%
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} The two surveys gave $36\%$ and $40\%$. Explain why that
        difference does \emph{not} mean somebody counted wrong.
        \writelines{2}

  \item The board's draft report says: \textit{``Harbor Point's $1{,}500$ members
        want a second lap lane.''} Every count behind it is correct.

        Explain \emph{two} separate things that sentence claims which the survey
        cannot support.
        \writelines{2}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.08in}

\boxguard
\begin{extensionbox}
\small
Pick a group you belong to --- a team, a class, a club, a workplace. Write a
question about it that data could settle, then fill in the plan:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item My question, and the population it is about.
  \item The sample I could realistically collect, and its size.
  \item The statistic I would compute, and the parameter it estimates.
  \item The one constraint that stops me from running a census.
\end{enumerate}
\end{extensionbox}
