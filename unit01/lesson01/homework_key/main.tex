\begin{scenariobox}[Lakeside Farmers Market]{cerulean}
\small
The Lakeside Farmers Market draws about $800$ shoppers on a Saturday morning. The
market manager is deciding whether to ask the town for a free shuttle from the
library parking lot, so she runs a study.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, labelindent=0pt, align=left, labelsep=0.6em,
                  itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt, label=\textbf{Stage \arabic*.}]
  \item She asks: \emph{``How do Lakeside Farmers Market shoppers travel to the
        market on a Saturday?''}
  \item One Saturday she asks $50$ shoppers as they arrive.
  \item She counts the answers by travel method.
  \item She writes up what the market should do next.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{2pt}
Her $50$ answers: $26$ drove, $12$ walked, $7$ biked, $5$ rode the bus.
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.08in}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{Practice}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=9pt, topsep=3pt]

  \item Name the \textbf{variable of interest} --- what the manager recorded for
        each shopper.

        \par\vspace{6pt}
        Variable: \ans{how that shopper traveled} \hfill Type: \ans{categorical}
        \par\vspace{10pt}
        How do you know? \ans{drove, walked, biked, bus are labels --- no mean}

  \item Complete the manager's frequency table.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
        \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} l c X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{How they traveled} & \textbf{Count} &
        \textbf{Relative frequency (percent)} \\
        \midrule
        Drove & 26 & \ans{$26/50 = 0.52 = 52\%$} \\
        Walked & 12 & \ans{$12/50 = 0.24 = 24\%$} \\
        Biked &  7 & \ans{$7/50 = 0.14 = 14\%$} \\
        Bus   &  5 & \ans{$5/50 = 0.10 = 10\%$} \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Total} & \ans{50} & \ans{$50/50 = 1.00 = 100\%$} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

        Show your work for the \textbf{Bus} row.

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{5}{50} &= 0.10 \\
                        &= 10\%
        \end{work}

  \item About $800$ shoppers come on a Saturday. Based on the survey, predict how
        many of them ride the bus to the market.

        \begin{work}
          0.10 \times 800 &= 80 \text{ shoppers}
        \end{work}

        Write that result as one sentence the manager could put in her report.
        Name the group and the number.
        \par\ansline{About 80 of the market's 800 Saturday shoppers ride the bus to the market.}

  \item The manager also asked eight of the shoppers how many miles they traveled
        to reach the market: $3, \; 5, \; 1, \; 8, \; 2, \; 6, \; 4, \; 3$.

        \textbf{(a)} Find the mean distance traveled.

        \begin{work}
          \bar{x} &= \dfrac{3+5+1+8+2+6+4+3}{8} \\
                  &= \dfrac{32}{8} \\
                  &= 4 \text{ miles}
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} Write one sentence interpreting that number. Name the group
        and the units.
        \par\ansline{These eight shoppers traveled a mean of 4 miles.}

        \textbf{(c)} Distance is quantitative, so a mean is available. Explain why
        the manager cannot report a ``mean travel method'' the same way.
        \par\ansline{Labels cannot be added, so no mean exists --- only counts.}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{Practice, continued}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=9pt, topsep=3pt, start=5]

  \item For each question, decide whether data could answer it as written. If not,
        name the test it fails and repair it.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
        \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} p{5.2cm} p{2cm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Question} & \textbf{Ready?} & \textbf{If not, repair it} \\
        \midrule
        Is the market popular?              & \ans{no} & \ans{How many shoppers visit on a Saturday morning?} \\
        How far did Mr.\ Ruiz drive today?  & \ans{no} & \ans{How far do Saturday shoppers drive to the market?} \\
        How many miles do Saturday shoppers travel to the market?
                                            & \ans{yes} & \ans{already passes all three tests} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item Each mistake below happens at one stage of the data cycle. Write the
        stage number.

        \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=4pt, topsep=3pt, label=(\alph*)]
          \item The manager asked only shoppers arriving in the parking lot.
                \hfill \ans{2}
          \item The 50 answers were never counted by travel method, so nobody
                could tell which group was biggest. \hfill \ans{3}
          \item The report says shoppers ``want a shuttle,'' but no one was ever
                asked about a shuttle. \hfill \ans{4}
          \item The original question never said \emph{which} shoppers or
                \emph{which} day. \hfill \ans{1}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item The manager's final report says: \textit{``Only $10\%$ of our shoppers
        ride the bus, so a free shuttle would be used by very few people.''}

        Every number in that sentence is correct. Explain why the conclusion still
        does not follow, and name the stage she should return to.
        \par\ansline{The survey recorded how shoppers travel now, not whether they would use a shuttle,}
        \ansline{and it missed anyone kept away by the trip. Go back to stage 1 and ask that.}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
