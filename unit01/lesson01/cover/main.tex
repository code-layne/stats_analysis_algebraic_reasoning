\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} name the four stages of the \textbf{data cycle} and explain what
        each stage hands to the next one.
  \item \ldots{} write a \textbf{statistical question} --- one whose answers vary,
        that names the group, and that says what will be measured.
  \item \ldots{} decide from the question alone whether the data I need are
        \textbf{categorical} or \textbf{quantitative}.
  \item \ldots{} organize counts into a \textbf{frequency table} and turn them
        into percents.
  \item \ldots{} write a conclusion in context, and say what the data do
        \emph{not} tell me.
  \item \ldots{} name the \textbf{stage} at which a flawed study broke down.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
The four stages run in order: \textbf{formulate the question}, \textbf{collect the
data}, \textbf{organize and represent}, \textbf{analyze and communicate} --- and
then the answer raises the next question. A conclusion may only describe the group
you actually collected data from, and it may only answer the question you actually
asked.
\end{remindbox}
