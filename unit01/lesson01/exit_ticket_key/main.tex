  \item The yearbook staff studied how students choose their senior quote. Write
        the stage number ($1$--$4$) beside each step.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.95\linewidth}{@{} X c @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{What the staff did} & \textbf{Stage} \\
        \midrule
        Counted the answers into a table by source of the quote. & \ans{3} \\
        Reported that most seniors used a quote from a song.     & \ans{4} \\
        Decided to ask seniors where their quote came from.      & \ans{1} \\
        Asked all 210 seniors the question.                      & \ans{2} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item A club adviser writes: \textit{``Do people like our meetings?''}

        \textbf{(a)} Name one test this question fails. \ans{it names no group}

        \textbf{(b)} Rewrite it so data could answer it.
        \ansline{How many club meetings did each Riverbend club member attend this fall?}

        \textbf{(c)} For your new question, what would you record for each
        person, and what type is it?

        Record: \ans{meetings attended} \hfill Type: \ans{quantitative}

  \item The staff surveyed only the seniors in the yearbook class, then wrote:
        \textit{``Riverbend seniors prefer song lyrics.''}

        Which stage broke down, and why does that stop the conclusion?
        \ansline{Stage 2. The data came only from yearbook students, not from seniors generally,}
        \ansline{so the conclusion applies to that class alone --- no wider claim is supported.}

\end{enumerate}
