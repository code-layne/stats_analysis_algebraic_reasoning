\begin{notesbox}{Spiral Review --- Percents, Scaling, and Types of Data}
\small
The Riverbend Student Council asked $60$ students, ``How do you usually get to
school?'' Of the $60$ students asked, $27$ said they ride the bus.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=6pt, topsep=4pt]

  \item What \textbf{percent} of the students asked ride the bus?

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{27}{60} &= 0.45 \\
                         &= 45\%
        \end{work}

  \item Riverbend has $900$ students in all. Based on this survey,
        \textbf{predict} how many of the $900$ students ride the bus.

        \begin{work}
          0.45 \times 900 &= 405 \text{ students}
        \end{work}

  \item The council recorded two things about each student. Classify each
        variable, and give the reason you used.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.95\linewidth}{@{} p{4.2cm} p{2.9cm} X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{How I know} \\
        \midrule
        How the student gets to school & \ans{categorical} & \ans{bus, car, walk, bike are labels} \\
        Minutes the trip takes         & \ans{quantitative} & \ans{an amount in minutes; a mean makes sense} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
