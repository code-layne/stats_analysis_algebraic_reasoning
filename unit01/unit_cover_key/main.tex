\begin{headlinebox}{cerulean}{\color{white}\bfseries Unit 1 --- Exam Scoring Notes (Teacher Copy)}\end{headlinebox}

\vspace{4pt}
\small
\noindent
Both Unit 1 assessments are parallel forms: same blueprint, same $100$ points
(Part A $20$, Part B $20$, Part C $40$, Part D $20$), $20$ items, different numbers,
contexts, and answer letters. One point split and one rubric serve both forms.
\normalsize

\begin{teachernote}[Part A \& Part B --- Answer Letters]
\scriptsize
\textbf{Practice Test.} Vocabulary (1--10): \textbf{D H G A C I E J F B}. \quad
Multiple choice (11--15): \textbf{C A D B A}.

\textbf{Unit Test.} Vocabulary (1--10): \textbf{F A D I G B J E C H}. \quad
Multiple choice (11--15): \textbf{B D A C D}.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Scoring.} Vocabulary $2$ points each; multiple choice $4$ points each, no partial
credit. The five MC items test, in order: quantitative vs.\ categorical (1.0), statistic vs.\
parameter (1.2), naming a sampling technique (1.3), the direction of a biased sample (1.4),
and experiment vs.\ observational study (1.5--1.6).
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Part D --- Rubric (both forms)]
\scriptsize
\textbf{20 --- is the principal's causal claim justified? ($20$).}
\textbf{No, with a reason} $6$: nobody was assigned, so it is an observational study.
\textbf{Association, not causation} $4$: the student uses the word, or says the data show only
that the two go together.
\textbf{Another variable} $6$: a plausible variable ($3$) \emph{and} how it could affect both
things being compared ($3$) --- family support, income, sleep, motivation, time at home.
\textbf{What would show causation} $4$: an experiment that randomly assigns the treatment.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Watch for.} ``The sample was too small'' or ``they should ask more students'' is the
wrong reason --- the sample was random; what is missing is \emph{assignment}. An answer that
says ``no'' with no reason earns at most $4$.
\end{teachernote}
