\begin{notesbox}{Exit Ticket --- No Notes}
\small
Bayside Middle School has $750$ students. $150$ of them are in band; the other
$600$ are not. Looking at last quarter's report cards, the principal finds that
$90$ of the band students earned an A or B in math, and $240$ of the students
not in band did.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=9pt, topsep=3pt]

  \item Students sign up for band themselves --- nobody was placed in it for
        this study.

        \textbf{(a)} Is this an observational study? \ans{yes}

        \textbf{(b)} How do you know? \ansline{The students chose band themselves; the school only looked at records.}

  \item Compute both rates.

        \textbf{(a)} What percent of the band students earned an A or B?

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{90}{150} &= 0.60 \\
                          &= 60\%
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} What percent of the students not in band did?

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{240}{600} &= 0.40 \\
                           &= 40\%
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(c)} The band students are higher by \ans{$20$} percentage
        points.

  \item The principal concludes: \emph{``Playing in band raises math grades, so
        every student should be required to take band.''} Say whether this study
        supports that, and name one other explanation for the gap.
        \ansline{No. Band is associated with higher math grades, but nobody was assigned to band.}
        \ansline{Students who sign up may already study more, or have families with more free time.}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
