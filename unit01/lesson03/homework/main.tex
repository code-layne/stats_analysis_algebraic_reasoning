\boxguard[24]
\begin{scenariobox}[Harbor Point Community Pool --- Do It Again, Properly]{cerulean}
\small
Last time, a staff member asked the $75$ members who arrived on a Tuesday
evening. The board has thrown that study out and hired you to plan a new one:
$100$ of the pool's $1{,}500$ members.

\vspace{2pt}
What the office knows: the membership list is complete and numbered $0001$ to
$1500$. There are $600$ individual memberships, $525$ family memberships, and
$375$ senior memberships. The week is divided into $25$ scheduled swim sessions,
and about $25$ members attend each one.
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.08in}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{Practice}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=9pt, topsep=3pt]

  \item The Tuesday-evening study was a \blank{4cm} sample.

        Name two kinds of members who had \emph{no chance} of being chosen by it.
        \writeline

  \item \textbf{Plan A --- simple random sample.} You number the members $0001$
        to $1500$ and run a random number generator. It returns:

        \begin{center}
        \ttfamily\large 0412 \quad 1587 \quad 0973 \quad 0412 \quad 0288
        \end{center}

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{} c X X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Returned} & \textbf{Use it? Why or why not} & \textbf{Member chosen} \\
        \midrule
        0412 & yes --- it is in range and new & member 412 \\
        1587 & \blank{4.2cm} & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        0973 & \blank{4.2cm} & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        0412 & \blank{4.2cm} & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        0288 & \blank{4.2cm} & \blank{2.6cm} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item \textbf{Plan B --- stratified sample} by membership type. The sample is
        $100$ of $1{,}500$ members, so take $1/15$ of each stratum.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.9\linewidth}{@{} l c X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Membership} & \textbf{Members} & \textbf{Number to survey} \\
        \midrule
        Individual & 600 & \blank{4cm} \\
        Family     & 525 & \blank{4cm} \\
        Senior     & 375 & \blank{4cm} \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Total} & \textbf{1,500} & \blank{4cm} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

        Show the work for the \textbf{senior} row.

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{100}{1500} &= \dfrac{1}{15} \\
          \dfrac{1}{15} \times 375 &= 25 \text{ members}
        \end{work}

  \item \textbf{Plan C --- systematic sample} from the numbered membership list.

        \textbf{(a)} Find the interval $k$.

        \begin{work}
          k &= 1500 \div 100 \\
            &= 15
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} The random start lands on member $9$. Write the next three
        members chosen.

        \blank{2cm} \qquad \blank{2cm} \qquad \blank{2cm}

  \item \textbf{Plan D --- cluster sample.} You draw $4$ of the $25$ swim
        sessions at random and survey everyone who attends them.

        \textbf{(a)} About how many members does that reach?

        \begin{work}
          4 \times 25 &= 100 \text{ members}
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} What are the clusters in this plan? \blank{5.5cm}

  \item Name the technique each study used.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.97\linewidth}{@{} X p{2.9cm} @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{The study} & \textbf{Technique} \\
        \midrule
        An airline randomly picks $5$ flights and surveys everyone on board.
          & \blank{2.5cm} \\
        A pollster has a complete voter list and draws $500$ numbers at random.
          & \blank{2.5cm} \\
        A bakery tests every $40$th loaf coming out of the oven.
          & \blank{2.5cm} \\
        A hospital surveys day-, evening-, and night-shift nurses in proportion.
          & \blank{2.5cm} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item The board says: ``Whatever we do, we have to be sure seniors are heard
        --- they pay dues too, and they swim at different hours.''

        Which of your four plans should they run, and why does it deliver what
        the board asked for?
        \writelines{2}

  \item Suppose the board runs \textbf{Plan D} and the four sessions drawn turn
        out to be three lap-swim hours and one adult fitness hour. The draw was
        completely random and every count is correct.

        Explain why the results still might not describe all $1{,}500$ members.
        \writelines{2}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.08in}

\boxguard
\begin{extensionbox}
\small
Pick a population you could actually study --- your school, your team, your
neighborhood. Write the question you would ask, then plan two different samples
of the same size:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item The frame you would use, and how you would number it.
  \item Plan 1: which technique, and exactly how chance chooses.
  \item Plan 2: a different technique, same sample size.
  \item Which plan you would actually run, and the one reason why.
\end{enumerate}
\end{extensionbox}
