\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} plan a survey through all four stages of the
        \textbf{statistical cycle}.
  \item \ldots{} build a \textbf{survey instrument} whose items are neutral and
        whose choices can be counted.
  \item \ldots{} \textbf{collect} real data from a real sample and organize it
        into a frequency table.
  \item \ldots{} choose and complete a \textbf{visual representation} that fits
        categorical data.
  \item \ldots{} report a conclusion \emph{in context} and judge its
        \textbf{validity} --- what my design does and does not let me claim.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
\textbf{This is the unit, run forwards.} Every lesson since 1.1 took apart a
study somebody else built; today you build one. \textbf{Formulate} a question
that names a group and a measurement, \textbf{collect} by choosing a sample and
writing a neutral instrument, \textbf{represent} the answers in a frequency
table and a bar graph, and \textbf{analyze and report} in context. The last
step is the one that decides your grade: a conclusion may only describe the
people your sample actually covers. Your class is a \textbf{convenience
sample}, so say so --- naming the limit is part of the answer, not an
apology for it.
\end{remindbox}
