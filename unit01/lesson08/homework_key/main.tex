\boxguard[20]
\begin{scenariobox}[Millbrook Public Library]{cerulean}
Millbrook has $1{,}200$ card holders and one grant to spend on a new program. The director draws
a \textbf{simple random sample of $80$} card holders from the membership list, reaches every one
by phone, and reads each the same item with four choices: evening book club $28$, teen coding
lab $24$, Saturday story hour $20$, job-search help $8$.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Meanwhile}, a volunteer leaves a sign-up clipboard on the front desk for a week. $50$
people write something down: book club $10$, coding lab $6$, story hour $30$, job-search help $4$.
\end{scenariobox}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{Represent, Analyze, Report, continued}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=0pt, start=3]

  \item Two surveys, one library, the same arithmetic.

        \textbf{(a)} Scale the phone survey's book-club percent to all $1{,}200$ card holders. \hfill\smash{\gradedblank[id=q3a, type=short, points=0.5, answer={420}, check=numeric]{1.8cm}{13.5pt}}

        \begin{work}
          0.35 \times 1200 &= 420 \text{ card holders}
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} What percent of the $50$ who signed the clipboard chose story hour? \hfill\smash{\gradedblank[id=q3b, type=short, points=0.5, answer={60}, check=numeric, display={60\%}]{1.8cm}{13.5pt}}

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{30}{50} &= 0.60 = 60\%
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(c)} One of these percents may be scaled to all $1{,}200$; one may not. Which, and why?
        \gradedresponse{\gradedblank[id=q3c, type=rubric, points=1, display={The 35\% may --- chance chose the 80 from the whole list. The 60\% may not: the 50 chose themselves by walking in that week, so it describes only the people who signed.}]{\linewidth}{2in}}

  \item Suppose the clipboard had shown $13$ of $50$ for story hour --- $26\%$, almost exactly the
        phone survey's $25\%$. Does that near-match make it trustworthy for all $1{,}200$? Explain.
        \gradedresponse{\gradedblank[id=q4, type=rubric, points=2, display={No. Chance never chose the 50, so a match is luck, not evidence --- the same clipboard could have missed by 35 points, as it actually did, and its method could not tell us.}]{\linewidth}{2in}}

  \item An early draft of the director's item read: \emph{``Wouldn't you love a coding lab for
        our teens?''} Name the instrument rule it breaks and the name Lesson 1.4 gave that
        bias, then repair it.
        \gradedresponse{\gradedblank[id=q5, type=rubric, points=2, display={Rule 2, neutral wording --- Lesson 1.4 called it a leading (response-bias) item. Repair: ``Which one of these four programs should the grant fund?''}]{\linewidth}{2in}}

  \item \textbf{Your project report.} From your class's tally in the notes, write the four
        sentences: what you asked and of whom; how the sample was chosen and how many answered;
        what you found (the two largest categories, as percents); what it means --- \emph{about
        the people your design actually covers}.
        \gradedresponse{\gradedblank[id=q6, type=rubric, points=2, display={We asked which one option the \$3,000 grant should fund, of Riverbend students. The sample was the 25 students in this room --- the schedule chose it, not chance; all 25 answered. Club funding led at 36\%, followed by field trips at 28\%. Of the 25 students in this class, most would fund clubs --- this class only, a convenience sample.}]{\linewidth}{2in}}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
