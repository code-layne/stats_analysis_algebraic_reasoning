\begin{remindbox}
\small
\textbf{In an experiment you assign --- you do not just watch.} The researcher
puts the experimental units into groups by \textbf{chance} and imposes a
\textbf{treatment} on at least one of them, while a \textbf{control group} shows
what happens without it. Four principles make the result worth trusting:
\textbf{comparison} (at least two groups), \textbf{randomization} (chance picks,
not people), \textbf{replication} (many units per group), and \textbf{control}
(everything else held the same). Because chance built the groups, this is the
one kind of study that earns the word \emph{caused}.
\end{remindbox}
