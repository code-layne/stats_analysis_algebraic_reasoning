\parthead{Part C --- Short Computations \hfill 4 questions, 10 points each}

\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=10pt, topsep=2pt, start=16]

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- C1 (1.2)
\item \textbf{Silver Lake Recreation Center} has $900$ members. The director asks
$60$ members chosen at random, and $18$ of them use the pool every week.

\vspace{2pt}
(a) What percent of the sample uses the pool every week? \hfill\smash{\gradedblank[id=q16a, type=short, points=5, answer={30}, check=numeric, display={30\%}]{1.6cm}{10pt}}

\begin{work}
  \dfrac{18}{60} &= 0.30 = 30\%
\end{work}

(b) About how many of all $900$ members use the pool every week? \hfill\smash{\gradedblank[id=q16b, type=short, points=5, answer={270}, check=numeric]{1.6cm}{10pt}}

\begin{work}
  0.30 \times 900 &= 270 \text{ members}
\end{work}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- C2 (1.3)

\item A random sample of Maplewood students found that students who \textbf{eat

students chose for themselves. The principal says, \emph{``Eating breakfast causes
higher grades.''}

\vspace{3pt}
Is the principal's conclusion justified? Explain your reasoning, and name one
\textbf{other variable} that could explain the result.

\par\gradedwriteline[{\gradedblank[id=q20, type=rubric, points=20, rubric=../rubrics/saar-u01-practice-test-q20.yaml, display={No. Nobody was assigned --- students chose --- so this is an observational study. Students with more support at home are more likely to eat breakfast \emph{and} to keep up with homework, and that support could raise grades by itself. Only an experiment that randomly assigns breakfast could show it causes higher grades.}]{\linewidth}{99.5pt}}]
