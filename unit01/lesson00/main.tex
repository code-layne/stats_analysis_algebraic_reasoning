\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=frost, colframe=cerulean, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will explain that data are numbers \emph{with a
    context}, identify the individuals and variables in a data set, classify each variable
    as categorical or quantitative, apply the digit test to a column of digits that cannot
    be averaged, and decide whether a question takes data to answer --- justifying every
    choice from the data it came from.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards:} PS.DC.1a, PS.DC.1b, PS.DC.1c; AFDA.DA.2a. \emph{Previews:} PS.DC.1d--e
    (1.2), PS.DC.2 (1.3), PS.DC.3 (1.5--1.7).
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.49\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
    \textbf{Skills for this lesson}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
      \item Read a data table and name the \textbf{individuals} (the rows) and the
            \textbf{variables} (the columns). \hfill \textit{PS.DC.1c; AFDA.DA.2a}
      \item Classify a variable as \textbf{categorical} or \textbf{quantitative},
            and justify the call. \hfill \textit{PS.DC.1c; AFDA.DA.2a}
      \item Explain why a number means nothing without its context ---
            who was measured, what was measured, and in what units.
            \hfill \textit{PS.DC.1a--b}
      \item Recognize and write a \textbf{statistical question} --- one whose
            answers are expected to vary. \hfill \textit{PS.DC.1b; AFDA.DA.2a}
      \item Summarize a categorical variable with counts and percents when a mean
            is not available. \hfill \textit{PS.DC.1a}
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.47\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
    \textbf{Key Understandings}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
      \item \emph{Data are collected for a purpose and have meaning in a context.}
            ``18'' answers nothing; ``the mean commute was 18 minutes for the six
            students surveyed'' answers something.
      \item The \textbf{type} of a variable decides what you are allowed to do with
            it. You may average a quantitative variable; a categorical variable can
            only be counted and turned into a percent.
      \item \textbf{Target misconception: the digit reflex.} Students classify
            anything written with digits as quantitative. Jersey number, grade level,
            and ZIP code are categorical. The test is not ``does it look like a
            number?'' but ``does arithmetic on it mean anything?''
      \item A \textbf{statistical question anticipates variability}. ``How tall am
            I?'' has one answer; ``How tall are students at this school?'' has many.
      \item A correct computation on the wrong kind of variable is still a worthless
            number. That is the sentence the whole lesson is built to earn.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries}p{3.1cm} X @{}}
Data & Numbers or labels collected about individuals, together with the context
       that gives them meaning. \\
Individual & One person, animal, or object that data are collected about ---
       one \emph{row} of a data table. \\
Variable & A characteristic recorded for each individual --- one \emph{column}
       of a data table. \\
Categorical variable & A variable that records a \emph{label} or group name
       (position, lunch choice, grade level, yes/no). Summarize with counts and percents. \\
Quantitative variable & A variable that records a \emph{measured amount} with
       units (points, minutes, siblings). Summarize with a mean or median. \\
Statistical question & A question whose answers are expected to \emph{vary} from
       individual to individual, so it takes data to answer. \\
Digit test & The check that settles a column written with digits: \emph{does
       arithmetic on this variable mean anything?} \\
Population & The entire group you want to know about. \emph{(Named formally in Lesson 1.2.)} \\
Sample & The part of the population you actually collect data from.
       \emph{(Named formally in Lesson 1.2.)} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{frost}
    Put the blog quote on the board before anyone sits down:
    \begin{center}
      \textit{``The Riverbend roster's mean jersey number is 14. That is low for a varsity
      squad --- this is a young, inexperienced team.''}
    \end{center}
    \textbf{Say:} every number in that sentence is correct. Show the roster and let a student
    verify $84/6 = 14$ on the board. \textbf{Then ask: is the \emph{conclusion} correct?}
    Take three or four answers and write them down without judging any of them --- ``the blog
    is biased,'' ``14 is not that low,'' ``jersey numbers don't mean anything.'' Circle the
    third one and tell the class that by the end of the notes they will be able to say
    \emph{why} it is the right objection. Do not resolve it here; section 4 is where it lands.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Lesson --- Guided Notes (20 min)]{frost}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{1. Data are numbers with a context (3 min).} Write $12$ on the board and ask what it
tells them. Nothing. Fill the who/what/units table, then read the finished sentence aloud:
\emph{``the six players on the Riverbend varsity roster scored a mean of 12 points per
player.''} That contrast --- bare number versus sentence --- is the whole section.

\textbf{2. Individuals and variables (3 min). \emph{This is the I-do section.}} Model it: put
your finger on Nia's row and say ``one individual''; run it across the Points column and say
``one variable.'' Count aloud: 6 and 5. Address ``Player'' explicitly --- it names the rows,
it is not a characteristic under study, so it is not counted.

\textbf{3. Two types of variables (5 min). \emph{We do.}} Fill the definition table first,
then classify all five roster columns \emph{as a class}, taking the reason from a student
every time. Do Position and Points first (easy, opposite poles), Height third, Captain fourth,
and \textbf{leave Jersey \# for last} --- take a hand vote on it, record the split, and move on
without settling it. Close with the $3/6 = 50\%$ captains computation: a categorical variable
does get summarized, just not with a mean.

\textbf{4. The digit test (5 min). \emph{The lesson.}} Now settle Jersey \#. The work block
gives $84/6 = 14$; ask \textbf{``find me the player who wears 14 on average.''} Wait for the
silence. Then write the test in the box: \emph{does arithmetic on this variable mean
anything?} Fill the three failure rows (jersey, ZIP, grade level), and close by pointing back
at Points and Height --- also digits, both average fine. \textbf{Digits are not the enemy.}

\textbf{5. Statistical questions (3 min).} Fill the ``vary'' blank, run the four O/M questions
as a fast whip-around, then have students write one rewrite. Take two aloud.

\textbf{Practice box (1--2 min, \emph{you do}).} Release it. The Student Life Survey is the
second run at the trap --- Grade is 10, 11, 9, 12 --- and the mean commute is already worked
in the packet, so students spend the time on (b) and (d), not on arithmetic.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Explicit Instruction: Classifying a Variable]{frost}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} p{0.46\linewidth} X @{}}
\begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Steps --- give these verbatim}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Read one entry in the column out loud as a \textbf{sentence} about one
        individual.
  \item Ask: is that entry an \textbf{amount of something}, with units?
  \item If yes $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{quantitative}; summarize with a mean.
  \item If no $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{categorical}; summarize with counts and percents.
  \item \textbf{If the column is written with digits, run the digit test:} compute
        the mean, then ask who or what that mean describes. If nothing, it is
        categorical.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
&
\begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Worked example --- the ``Jersey \#'' column}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item[1.] ``Nia's jersey number is 12.''
  \item[2.] Is 12 an amount of anything? She does not have 12 of something.
  \item[3.] Digits, so run the test: $84/6 = 14$.
  \item[4.] Who wears 14 on average? \textbf{Nobody.} The mean describes no player
        and no quantity.
  \item[5.] \textbf{Categorical.} Summarize instead with counts --- how many players
        wear a number under 20.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Contrast: ``Height (in)'' is also digits. $420/6 = 70$ inches, and that
\emph{is} an amount --- the typical height of a player on this roster.
\textbf{Quantitative.}}
\end{minipage}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Watch for the digit reflex.} Notes section 3 and activity item 2, the
        Bib \# row. The most common error today by a wide margin. Ask: ``what would the
        average of these be, and who would that describe?''
  \item \textbf{Watch for reasons that are not reasons.} ``It's words'' and ``it's numbers''
        are the two answers to reject all period --- they are exactly the reasoning the digit
        test replaces. Require the reason to name an amount or a label.
  \item \textbf{Watch for over-correction after section 4.} Once Jersey \# is settled, some
        students reclassify Height and Finish time as categorical too. Ask whether 70 inches
        is an amount of anything.
  \item \textbf{Watch for context-free answers.} Notes practice (d), activity item 5. A student
        who writes ``18'' or ``24'' and stops has not finished. Require the group and the units.
  \item \textbf{Cold-call prompts:} ``Name one individual in this table.'' ``Name one
        variable.'' ``Give me a column of digits that is \emph{not} quantitative --- and one
        that is.'' ``Change this question so it takes data to answer.''
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Group Work (18 min)]{redbox}
\small
\textbf{One activity, the whole class, groups of three.} Everyone works items 1--7 off the same
meet card; the gold \emph{Extension} box at the end is for groups that get there, not a separate
assignment. Hand it out and start the clock --- there is nothing to assign or sort.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{The arc.} Items 1--2 are readable straight off the card and get every group writing
inside two minutes. Items 3--5 compute a mean and a percent (both work blocks) and demand a
sentence that names the group and the units. \textbf{Item 6 is the crux}: the blog's mean bib
number of $23$ is arithmetically correct and completely worthless, and 6(b) forces the group back
to the type they assigned in item 2. Item 7 is the hardest thing on the page --- the coach is
\emph{right about Team color for the wrong reason} --- and it is the item that most reliably
produces a usable board answer.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Circulate with questions, not answers.} \emph{Item 2, Bib \# row:} ``Say it in a
sentence --- \emph{Cruz's bib number is 45.} Is 45 an amount of something?'' \emph{Item 6(b),
if they defend the blog:} ``Compute it: 23. Now find me the runner who wears 23 on average.''
\emph{Item 6(b), if they are stuck at `it's wrong':} ``Wrong how? Point at the row in item 2
that decides it.'' \emph{Item 5 or 7, if the answer is bare:} ``Who does that describe?''
\emph{Extension item 1:} ``Who exactly did we measure?''

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Pick the work to display for the debrief:} one item 2 table with a defended Bib \# row,
one bare and one complete item 5 sentence, two different item 6(b) answers, one item 7, and --- if
any group got there --- one extension item 2.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (7 min) --- what goes on the board]{frost}
Run the displayed work in this order. \textbf{Students correct their own reasons in a second
color} as you go; that self-correction is the record you collect from.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Item 5.} Read one context-free answer and one complete one. Ask which
        one a coach could act on. Re-state: a number is not an answer until it names a group
        and units.
  \item \textbf{Item 6(b) --- the crux.} Put both displayed answers up. The arithmetic is
        correct in each. Land the rule out loud: \emph{a correct computation on the wrong kind
        of variable is still a worthless number.}
  \item \textbf{Item 7.} The coach got Team color right for the wrong reason. Have the
        class state the digit test in their own words. \textbf{This is the check for
        understanding} --- if the room cannot state it here, reopen it at the top of 1.1.
  \item \textbf{Extension item 2}, if any group reached it --- otherwise pose it aloud. Same
        runners, two questions, two types. Close on it: the
        \emph{question} decides the type, not the people and not the digits.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Formative read.} As they self-correct, sort the room into three piles: \textbf{(a)}
classified correctly \emph{and} justified from amount-versus-label; \textbf{(b)} classified
correctly but justified by ``it's a number'' / ``it's words''; \textbf{(c)} missed the
classification. Pile (b) is the biggest on day one and the most fixable --- that is the normal
starting point, not a failure of the lesson. Open Lesson 1.1 by reading two pile-(b) reasons
aloud, unattributed.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework --- Northgate Community Recreation Center.} A new context on the same spine,
seven items, about 20 minutes. Items 1--2 name individuals and variables and classify four
columns; the \textbf{contrast pair} is \emph{Member ID} (digits, label) against \emph{Age}
(digits, amount), which is the digit test with the training wheels off. Item 3 computes a mean
and \emph{interprets} it; item 4 justifies why a percent, not a mean, summarizes ``Group
class?''; item 5 is the \textbf{spiral} (Algebra 1 percent and proportion, scaled to 2000
members); item 6 sorts four questions into statistical and not, then rewrites one; item 7 is
the \textbf{justification item} --- the mean Member ID of $140.5$ --- and cannot be answered by
computing anything. \textbf{Scored} out of 20, due at the start of the next class. Score items
1--5 for correctness and items 6--7 for the \emph{reason}, not the verdict.

\textbf{Packet or DeltaMath --- decide this lesson.} The packet set is authored either way.
DeltaMath's coverage of individuals/variables and categorical-vs-quantitative classification is
thin, and it has nothing for item 7's ``this mean measures nothing'' reasoning, so \textbf{assign
the packet pages for 1.0}. Say which one aloud at Close \& Assign so it is unambiguous.

\textbf{Extension (optional).} Students bring in one real claim about a group of people --- a
headline, an ad, a post --- and name the variable behind it and its type. Collect these; the
strongest ones are reused in Lesson 1.4 for bias.

\textbf{Preview of Lesson 1.1.} We name the four stages of the data cycle formally, write
questions a data set can actually answer, and organize collected answers into a frequency
table. Today's words --- individual, variable, categorical, quantitative --- are assumed from
here on.

\textbf{Connections \& Big Ideas.} \textit{Unit 1 core idea:} a conclusion is only as
trustworthy as the study that produced it. \textit{Standards covered:} PS.DC.1a, PS.DC.1b,
PS.DC.1c; AFDA.DA.2a. \textit{Spirals forward to:} population vs.\ sample and parameter vs.\
statistic (1.2, PS.DC.1d--e), sampling technique (1.3, PS.DC.2), bias (1.4), and observational
studies vs.\ experiments (1.5--1.7, PS.DC.3). \textit{Reaches back to:} Algebra 1 percent and
proportional reasoning.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
    Five minutes, silent, timed. All three items are Algebra 1 review and should be quick; the
    point is diagnostic, not instructional. Item 2 (scaling $18/50$ up to 900 students) is the
    one that separates the class --- students who multiply $18 \times 18$ or add rather than
    scale the \emph{proportion} need a five-minute pull-aside during the group activity. Item 3
    asks for the mean \emph{and} a sentence naming what it describes; do not accept the bare
    number, because notes section 1 turns on exactly that sentence.

    \textbf{If it runs long, cut item 1} --- the $3/6 = 50\%$ captains computation in notes
    section 3 re-teaches the same percent in context ten minutes later.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
    Pace: section 1 in 3 minutes, section 2 in 3, section 3 in 5, section 4 in 5, section 5 in
    3, and release the practice box for the last 1--2. \textbf{Section 4 is the lesson}; the
    other four exist to earn it. If you are behind, compress section 5 --- the O/M sort is
    reinforced again in homework item 6 --- but never
    section 4.

    The single highest-leverage move today is in section 3: classify Jersey \# \emph{last},
    take a hand vote, record the split on the board, and then \textbf{do not settle it}. The
    digit test in section 4 lands because students have already committed to a side and watched
    a correct computation produce a worthless number. If you resolve Jersey \# during section 3,
    section 4 becomes a definition to copy rather than an argument to win, and the misconception
    survives the lesson.

    The practice box's mean commute is pre-worked in the packet ($108/6 = 18$), so the release
    is about (b) --- \emph{Grade} is the second instance of the trap --- and (d), the
    interpretation sentence. Make a student, not you, compute the mean grade level aloud
    ($63/6 = 10.5$) and ask who is in grade $10.5$.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Group Activity]
    Groups of three, 18 minutes, one activity for everyone. No tiers, nothing to assign --- hand
    it out and start the clock. The \emph{Extension} box is the release valve for groups that
    finish items 1--7 early; expect two or three groups to reach it and none to finish it, which
    is the intent.

    Announce a 10-minute mark. Groups should be at item 5 by then; \textbf{item 6(b) is the crux
    and no group should leave without an answer on the page}, right or wrong. If the room is
    running slow, say aloud that items 3 and 4 have their arithmetic pre-worked in the packet and
    that the sentence in item 5 is what you are collecting --- that usually recovers four minutes.

    Item 7 is worth circulating to twice. ``Right answer, wrong reason'' is harder than it looks:
    groups tend to agree with the coach's verdict and stop reading. Ask them which half of the
    coach's sentence they are agreeing with.

    Extension item 1 asks for \emph{two} overclaims and students typically find one (the five
    runners are not the whole squad). The second is ``fastest in the district'' --- no other team
    was ever measured. If no group reaches the extension, pose item 2 aloud in the debrief; it is
    the cleanest close the lesson has.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
    Assign all seven items. Item 3 asks for a mean \emph{and} a sentence interpreting it, and
    item 7 cannot be answered by computing anything --- flag both when assigning, because
    students who treat this as a calculation sheet will skip exactly those. Item 5 is the
    Algebra 1 spiral and is the one to check first if the warm-up showed scaling trouble.

    Grade item 7 first and only item 7 if time is short: it is the single item that separates
    students who learned the rule from students who learned the reason. Any answer that
    computes something \emph{about} $140.5$ is a miss, however well written. Go over item 2's
    Member ID row and item 7 at the start of Lesson 1.1, in that order.

    DeltaMath does not carry this lesson's skills adequately (see the Reinforcement box), so the
    packet pages are the assignment for 1.0. The optional extension is worth collecting even
    from students who skip it --- those artifacts are reused in Lesson 1.4 for bias.
\end{teachernote}
