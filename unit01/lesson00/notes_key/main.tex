\begin{objectivebox}
\small
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\dots
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item explain why a number means nothing until I know \emph{who} was measured,
        \emph{what} was measured, and in \emph{what units};
  \item read a data table and name the \textbf{individuals} and the
        \textbf{variables};
  \item classify a variable as \textbf{categorical} or \textbf{quantitative}, and
        justify the call;
  \item apply the \textbf{digit test} to a column written with digits that still
        cannot be averaged;
  \item recognize and write a \textbf{statistical question}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\boxguard
\begin{vocabbox}
\small
Fill in each term as we build it together.
\vocabans{Data}{Numbers or labels collected about individuals, together with the
context --- who, what, and the units --- that gives them meaning.}
\vocabans{Individual}{One person, animal, or object that data are collected
about; one row of a data table.}
\vocabans{Variable}{A characteristic recorded for each individual; one column of
a data table.}
\vocabans{Categorical variable}{A variable that records a label or group name.
Summarize it with counts and percents.}
\vocabans{Quantitative variable}{A variable that records a measured amount with
units. Summarize it with a mean or a median.}
\vocabans{Statistical question}{A question whose answers are expected to vary
from individual to individual, so it takes data from many individuals to answer.}
\end{vocabbox}

\boxguard[12]
\begin{hookbox}
\small
A sports blog wrote this about the Riverbend varsity basketball team:

\begin{center}
\textit{``The Riverbend roster's mean jersey number is 14. That is low for a
varsity squad --- this is a young, inexperienced team.''}
\end{center}

The arithmetic in that sentence is \textbf{correct}. Is the conclusion?
\ansline{No. Jersey numbers are labels, not amounts, so their mean measures nothing.}
\ansline{A correct computation on the wrong kind of variable is still a worthless number.}
\end{hookbox}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{notesbox}{1. Data Are Numbers With a Context}
\small
Write the number $12$ on your paper. What does it tell you? \ans{nothing at all}

\vspace{3pt}
Nothing --- and it never will, until three things come with it. \textbf{Data} are
numbers or labels together with the context that gives them meaning:

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Who:} \ans{the individuals the data were collected about}

\textbf{What:} \ans{the characteristic that was recorded for each one}

\textbf{Units:} \ans{what the numbers are counted or measured in}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Say it as a sentence:} ``The six players on the Riverbend varsity roster
scored a mean of \ans{12} \ans{points} per player.'' \emph{That} is data.
The bare $12$ is not.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{2. Individuals and Variables}
\small
Here is the roster the blog was reading.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{@{} l c l c c c @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Player} & \textbf{Jersey \#} & \textbf{Position} & \textbf{Points} &
\textbf{Height (in)} & \textbf{Captain} \\
\midrule
Nia    & 12 & Guard   & 14 & 64 & Yes \\
Ruben  &  7 & Forward &  9 & 71 & No  \\
Sasha  & 23 & Center  &  6 & 75 & No  \\
Marcus &  4 & Guard   & 19 & 68 & Yes \\
Talia  & 17 & Forward & 11 & 70 & Yes \\
Devon  & 21 & Guard   & 13 & 72 & No  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
Every data table is built the same way:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{0.95\linewidth}{@{} p{2.4cm} p{4.1cm} X @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Each row} & is one \ans{individual} & \ans{one player on this roster} \\
\textbf{Each column} & is one \ans{variable} & \ans{one thing recorded about every player} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
This table has \ans{6} individuals. Not counting the ``Player'' column,
which only names them, it records \ans{5} variables.

\vspace{2pt}
\textit{``Player'' is a label that tells the rows apart. It is not a
characteristic we are studying, so we do not count it as a variable.}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[36]
\begin{notesbox}{3. Two Types of Variables}
\small
The \textbf{type} of a variable decides what you are allowed to do with it.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabularx}{0.97\linewidth}{@{} p{3.1cm} X p{3.2cm} @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Type} & \textbf{Records} & \textbf{Summarize with} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Quantitative} & a measured \ans{amount} with units & \ans{a mean} \\
\textbf{Categorical}  & a \ans{label} or group name & counts and \ans{percents} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
Classify each recorded column of the roster. In the last column, say \emph{how you
decided} --- not ``it's words'' or ``it's numbers.''
\nopagebreak

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{0.97\linewidth}{@{} p{2.6cm} p{3.2cm} X @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{How I decided} \\
\midrule
Jersey \#   & \ans{categorical} & \ans{it names a player; averaging it means nothing} \\
Position    & \ans{categorical} & \ans{Guard and Forward are group names} \\
Points      & \ans{quantitative} & \ans{it counts an amount scored, in points} \\
Height (in) & \ans{quantitative} & \ans{it measures an amount, in inches} \\
Captain     & \ans{categorical} & \ans{Yes/No are labels, not amounts} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
A mean is not available for ``Captain,'' so summarize it the other way. What
percent of these six players are captains?

\begin{work}
  \dfrac{3}{6} &= 0.50 \\
               &= 50\%
\end{work}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{4. The Digit Test}
\small
The blog averaged the jersey numbers. Check it:

\begin{work}
  \bar{x} &= \dfrac{12+7+23+4+17+21}{6} \\
          &= \dfrac{84}{6} \\
          &= 14
\end{work}

\vspace{2pt}
The arithmetic is right. Now find me the player who wears $14$ on average.
\ans{There is none.}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{The test is not} ``does it look like a number?'' \textbf{The test is:}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.86\linewidth}{\centering \vspace{2pt}
Does \ans{doing arithmetic} on this variable mean anything? \vspace{2pt}}}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
Three classic columns of digits that fail the test:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{0.9\linewidth}{@{} p{3.4cm} X @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Column of digits} & \textbf{Why the mean is worthless} \\
\midrule
Jersey number & \ans{it identifies a player; nobody wears the average number} \\
ZIP code      & \ans{it identifies a place; the average of two ZIPs is not between them} \\
Grade level   & \ans{it names a group; there is no student in grade 10.5} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
\textit{Digits are not the enemy. Meaningless arithmetic is: ``Points'' and
``Height'' are digits too, and both of them average just fine.}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{5. Questions That Take Data to Answer}
\small
A \textbf{statistical question} is one whose answers are expected to
\ans{vary} from individual to individual --- so it takes data from
\emph{many} individuals to answer.

\vspace{4pt}
The athletic director has four questions. Mark each \textbf{O} if \emph{one}
look-up answers it, or \textbf{M} if it takes answers from \emph{many} players.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=4pt, topsep=3pt, label=(\roman*)]
  \item What number does Sasha wear? \hfill \ans{O}
  \item How many points do Riverbend players score in a game? \hfill \ans{M}
  \item How tall is Marcus? \hfill \ans{O}
  \item How tall are the players on the Riverbend roster? \hfill \ans{M}
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4pt}
The ones you marked \textbf{M} are the statistical questions. Take one you marked
\textbf{O} and rewrite it so that answering it would take data from many players.
\ansline{What numbers do the Riverbend players wear? (Also accept: how tall are Riverbend guards?)}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard
\begin{practicebox}
\small
Six Riverbend students filled out the Student Life Survey.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.1}
\begin{tabular}{@{} l c c l c c @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Student} & \textbf{Grade} & \textbf{Commute (min)} & \textbf{Lunch} &
\textbf{Siblings} & \textbf{Club member} \\
\midrule
Alina   & 10 & 20 & Home       & 2 & Yes \\
Devon   & 11 &  8 & Cafeteria  & 0 & No  \\
Marisol &  9 & 35 & Home       & 3 & Yes \\
Theo    & 12 & 15 & Cafeteria  & 1 & Yes \\
Priya   & 10 &  5 & Off campus & 2 & No  \\
Jamal   & 11 & 25 & Cafeteria  & 4 & Yes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{(a)} This table has \ans{6} individuals and \ans{5}
variables.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{(b)} Classify: Commute is \ans{quantitative}; Lunch is \ans{categorical};
Grade is \ans{categorical}, even though it is written with digits.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{(c)} Find the mean commute time for these six students.

\begin{work}
  \bar{x} &= \dfrac{20+8+35+15+5+25}{6} \\
          &= \dfrac{108}{6} \\
          &= 18 \text{ minutes}
\end{work}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{(d)} Write one sentence interpreting your answer to (c). Name the group
and the units.
\ansline{The six students surveyed had a mean one-way commute of 18 minutes.}
\end{practicebox}
