\begin{notesbox}{Practice}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=9pt, topsep=3pt]

  \item How many \textbf{individuals} does this table describe? \ans{8}

        How many \textbf{variables} does it record? \ans{5}

        (Remember: the ``Member'' column names the individuals --- it is not a
        variable being studied.)

  \item Classify each variable, and give the reason you used.

        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.96\linewidth}{@{} p{3.2cm} p{3cm} X @{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{How I know} \\
        \midrule
        Member ID          & \ans{categorical}  & \ans{a label written with digits; a mean ID means nothing} \\
        Membership         & \ans{categorical}  & \ans{Family / Student / Senior are group names} \\
        Visits last month  & \ans{quantitative} & \ans{a count of visits; a mean makes sense} \\
        Age                & \ans{quantitative} & \ans{an amount measured in years} \\
        \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item Find the mean number of visits last month.

        \begin{work}
          \bar{x} &= \dfrac{6+2+9+4+14+1+8+4}{8} \\
                  &= \dfrac{48}{8} \\
                  &= 6 \text{ visits}
        \end{work}

        Now write one sentence interpreting that number. Name the group and the
        units.
        \ansline{These eight Northgate members made a mean of 6 visits during last month.}

  \item ``Group class?'' is categorical, so a mean is not available. Find the
        \textbf{percent} of these eight members who take a group class.

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{5}{8} &= 0.625 \\
                       &= 62.5\%
        \end{work}

        Explain why a percent, and not a mean, is the right summary here.
        \ansline{The values are Yes and No labels, which cannot be added --- they can only be counted.}

  \item Northgate has $2000$ members in all. A survey of $300$ of them found that
        $45$ use the swimming pool.

        \textbf{(a)} What percent of the members surveyed use the pool?

        \begin{work}
          \dfrac{45}{300} &= 0.15 \\
                          &= 15\%
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(b)} Based on the survey, predict how many of the $2000$ members
        use the pool.

        \begin{work}
          0.15 \times 2000 &= 300 \text{ members}
        \end{work}

        \textbf{(c)} Write the result as one sentence the recreation center could
        put in a report.
        \ansline{About 300 of Northgate's 2000 members use the swimming pool.}

  \item Mark each question \textbf{S} (statistical) or \textbf{N} (not
        statistical).

        \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=4pt, topsep=3pt, label=(\alph*)]
          \item What time does Northgate open on Saturday? \hfill \ans{N}
          \item How old are Northgate's members? \hfill \ans{S}
          \item How many visits did Tam make last month? \hfill \ans{N}
          \item How many visits do Northgate members make in a month?
                \hfill \ans{S}
        \end{enumerate}

        Rewrite part (c) so that it becomes a statistical question.
        \ansline{How many visits do Northgate's student members make in a month?}

  \item A staff member computes the mean of the Member ID column and gets
        $140.5$. She writes: \textit{``The average Northgate member is number
        $140.5$.''}

        What does that number actually tell you about Northgate's members?
        Explain.
        \ansline{Nothing. Member ID is a label used to tell members apart, not an amount,}
        \ansline{so its mean is not a measurement of anything about the members.}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{extensionbox}
\small
Find one real claim about a group of people --- a headline, an advertisement, a
poll on social media. Write it down, then answer three questions about it:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item What group does the claim say it describes?
  \item What variable was recorded to produce it?
  \item Is that variable categorical or quantitative?
\end{enumerate}

Bring the claim to class --- we will come back to these when we study bias.
\end{extensionbox}
